# Parallel streams may observe one another

> **Document control:** ADR-006 · Accepted · Custodian: Repository maintainer

**Decision source:** the owner, 2026-10-20, in the working session that opened
[GOV-008](../governance/gov-008-human-like-learning-motivation.md). This record
transcribes that instruction. It records a permission and its limits; it does
not choose a mechanism.

## Context

SkyBot runs several parallel streams of one shared brain. Today the streams
write to shared replay, so they contribute to one world model, but no stream
observes or responds to another during acting. The owner wants cooperation and
collective learning, which the human-learning map treats as observation of peers.

## Decision

Streams of the shared brain **may observe one another** to support cooperation
and collective learning.

**Limits that continue to apply:**

- [ADR-002](adr-002-evaluator-state-is-not-a-learning-input.md): what one stream
  observes of another must be what a player in that position could perceive
  (pixels, sound, visible behaviour). Engine state, coordinates or counters of a
  peer are not a learning input.
- [ADR-001](adr-001-shared-universal-brain.md): the streams remain one brain, in
  every game. Observation is not a separate learner.
- No reward is paid for imitating a peer by itself; a mechanism must be judged
  by the adaptability metric ([ASS-007](../assurance/ass-007-adaptability-evaluation-protocol.md)),
  not by a peer-agreement score.

## Consequences and limits

- **What is observed, how it is used, and what it costs are open.** They are
  decided in a research design (GOV-008 work package WP-12) and tested by its
  own A/B before any benefit is claimed.
- Observation raises the compute cost per stream; [ADR-005](adr-005-compute-pressure-order.md)
  governs what yields.
- The risk to record: collective learning can also collapse diversity (all
  streams learning the same habit). The A/B must measure diversity of visited
  states as well as progress.

## Amendment 1 (2026-10-20, same day)

Source: the owner's second round of answers.

A stream may observe a peer's **chosen actions** as well as its visible frames
and sound. The limits above still apply: what is observed of a peer must be
what a player watching it could perceive, which includes the button inputs it
presses and the moves it makes, and excludes the peer's engine state or counters
(ADR-002). **Learned, not declared** also applies: observing a peer's actions
does not declare any action to be good, and no reward is paid for matching a
peer. Whether peer actions are used as a signal for prediction, for imitation
learned from experience, or only as context is a research-design question for
WP-12, tested by its own A/B.

## Amendment 2 (2026-10-21, response to the branch audit)

Source: [ASS-009](../assurance/ass-009-audit-response-adjustments-branch.md), finding
3.10. The assistant's judgement; revisable by the owner.

**Observation geometry.** Streams are **separate game instances**. A stream watches
another instance, as one watches another player's screen; they do not share a world.
The owner's "cooperation and collective learning" therefore means collective learning
across instances. Cooperation inside one shared world needs a multiplayer mode and is
deferred (RES-007 H-11).

**What a stream observes of a peer:** the peer's recent frames and sound, and the
actions it chose (as button inputs), over a short window **aligned to the observer's
decision count**, with the lag recorded. A peer is identified only by an anonymous slot
index.

**What a stream does not receive:** the peer's reward, its counters, its engine state,
its probation or success flags, or any label that says its action was good.

**Peer-derived effects are hypotheses, not labels.** A peer's observed effect may
propose a candidate effect to the observer (RW-9), but the observer must verify it
through its own probation (WP-1) before using it. No example is weighted by the peer's
success.

**Diversity** is measured in the shared view-embedding space (coverage of visited views
per stream), never from engine state.

**Leakage tests:** the peer channel carries no reward, flag or counter field; a test
fails otherwise.
